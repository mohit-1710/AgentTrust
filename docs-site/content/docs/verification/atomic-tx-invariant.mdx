---
title: "Asynchronous settlement invariants"
description: "Authorization does not make transfer and callback one all-or-nothing operation."
---

> This page describes the frozen native design. Check [implementation status](/reference/changelog) before using an integration; a planned interface is not a deployed service.

The native design reserves before scheduling, but the transfer and its callback are separate receipts. Authorization, successful payout and recorded settlement must remain distinguishable.

Required invariants include:

1. No new dispatch before authenticated invoice, policy, capability, trust and backing checks pass.
2. Reserve money and dispatch capacity before the native transfer is scheduled.
3. Definitive transfer failure restores the available claim at most once and retains the dispatch count.
4. Transfer success followed by callback failure leaves Pending and its reservation intact.
5. Duplicate requests/callbacks cannot cause a second payout, refund or finalization.
6. Timeouts and `needs_reconciliation` do not release money or justify a new ID.
7. Backing checks use actual balance and consistent post-storage liabilities, including the case where a claim is restored before its refund arrives.

Callbacks require the core account as predecessor and the expected promise result. A caller's owner/issuer label cannot grant callback authority.

Runtime evidence must show the relevant receipt graph and balance/storage observations. A final snapshot alone may not establish intermediate promise/refund timing. Any deliberately fault-injected test artifact must be labeled separately from the normal contract artifact.

The prototype has no general historical-outcome proof verifier or administrative finalizer. Pending records can remain locked; this is a disclosed liveness limit.
