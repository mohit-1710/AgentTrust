---
title: "Service and credential registry"
description: "Merchant-owned identity versions and issuer-owned capability assertions in the core."
---

The registry is part of the single native core. It stores two different records.

## Service identity

`set_service(expected_version, payout_account, invoice_key, endpoint_hash, active)` derives the service ID from the direct merchant caller. Creation requires `expected_version=null` and starts at version 1. Updating requires the exact current version and increments it, including an active-state update.

The invoice key is a raw 32-byte Ed25519 key in canonical padded base64. It is distinct from the complete native public key used for a worker session. The endpoint field is a hash commitment, not a discoverable URL.

## Credential assertion

`issue_credential(credential_id, expected_revision, assertion)` derives the issuer from the caller. The assertion contains service ID/version, capability namespace/version, suite version/hash, separate evidence-manifest hash and issue/expiry timestamps.

The referenced service must exist, be active and have the exact version. The supported capability is `invoice.audit` version `1`. Suite version is positive. Expiry is strictly after issue and current time; issue time has at most five seconds of clock-skew tolerance. This is not a hidden extension of expiry.

A new credential starts at revision 1. Revocation keeps that assertion revision and sets `revoked=true`; repeating it is a no-op. Renewal requires the expected revision, increments it and clears revoked. Old references can therefore distinguish `REVOKED` from `REVISION_CHANGED`.

At most 32 credentials are retained per issuer, including revoked records. Each actor funds its own storage. A caller cannot alter a record under another merchant or issuer account.

Read `get_service(service_id)` and `get_credential(issuer_id, credential_id)` for current records. Being registered or credentialed does not imply owner approval or spending authority.
